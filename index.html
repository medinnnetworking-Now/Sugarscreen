How it works

From findings to a plan

  1. 1

    Add a patient and record consent

    Name, age, sex and an optional patient ID. No module opens until consent is ticked.

  2. 2

    Work through the modules

    Type values, tick findings or take photos of the fundus, dipstick and wound. Results update as you type.

  3. 3

    Read the report

    One page with a status per organ, suggested actions, the next screening date and change since the last visit. Print or save as PDF.

Evidence

Built on published guidelines

  • ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes: glycaemic targets, CKD, retinopathy, neuropathy and foot care
  • ICO Guidelines for Diabetic Eye Care and the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) severity scale
  • KDIGO 2022 Diabetes Management in CKD and KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline; CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine equation
  • IWGDF 2023 guidelines: risk stratification, offloading, PAD and infection (with IDSA); Wagner and SINBAD classifications
  • RSSDI clinical practice recommendations; WHO Asia-Pacific BMI cut-offs
  • Ipswich Touch Test (Rayman et al., 2011); automated CCM thresholds (Perkins et al., 2018)

About

Complication screening that fits a clinic visit.

DiaScreen brings the checks recommended for people with diabetes into one workflow. It is a prototype for evaluation and teaching, built by {{ site.author.name }} ({{ site.author.role }}).

Privacy and safety

  • Patient records and photos are stored only in the browser on your device. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • You are signed out after 15 minutes of inactivity.
  • The app uses a shared demo login. It is not real authentication, so do not enter identifiable patient data on a shared or public device.
  • Not a certified medical device. A clinical deployment needs individual accounts, encrypted storage, audit logs and regulatory approval.
